01

What Midas is

Midas is a from-scratch rebuild of a Kalshi prediction-market trading bot as a multi-service, event-driven system on DigitalOcean Kubernetes — inspired by how hedge funds separate order management, portfolio management, and risk into distinct systems. The legacy bot (~5,300 lines, audited April 2026) had structural bugs (bankroll only went up, dead reconciliation, dead strategies, magic numbers everywhere). Midas is built deliberately to avoid those, governed by 10 binding constraints (see §10).

The legacy bot is paused, not actively developed. All current work is on Midas. Realistic timeline: 5–6 months across 8 phases.

Where we are in the v2 architecture (orientation)

Kitchen analogy. The cluster is a professional kitchen. DigitalOcean is the landlord; Kubernetes is the head chef deciding what runs where; Argo CD is the foreman who reads the cookbook (git) and makes the kitchen match it; sealed-secrets is the locked pantry; GHCR is the freezer aisle of pre-made meals (container images); NATS is the intercom every station shouts orders across; Postgres is the ledger; the LGTM stack is the CCTV.

As of tonight, the kitchen is fully equipped and has its first working cook: data-svc, the prep cook at the door receiving Kalshi deliveries and shouting them over the intercom (publishing ticks to NATS). It's live and verified.

06

Decisions on record (this session)

  • Sealed secrets colocate with their service in deploy/<svc>/ (one Argo path), superseding the earlier deploy/secrets/<svc>/ convention.
  • Kalshi prices are stored as Decimal cents (0–100) in MarketTickEvent; the live API sends dollar strings, so data-svc converts ×100 via Decimal (never float).
  • NATS subject is events.market.tick.<exchange>.<ticker> (permanent).
  • Behavioral changes ship as separate tested PRs (not fixes-while-porting); failing specs use xfail(strict=True) to keep main green.

10

The 10 binding constraints

  1. Bankroll only goes up → use event sourcing, not mutable balance fields
  2. Reconciliation never ran → must be on a scheduler
  3. Strategies as strings → ClassVar + __init_subclass__ enforcement
  4. Dead strategies → delete what isn't deployed
  5. Risk gates duplicated 8× → one canonical implementation
  6. Shadow drifted into research lab → paper environment ≠ research
  7. State file 34 keys deep, no schema → Pydantic + JSON Schema, versioned
  8. The learning system never learned → every event has a reader (and: verify with evidence, don't infer)
  9. Magic numbers everywhere → config in YAML, not code
  10. The bot grew faster than its tests → tests not optional, CI gates
